Expert Panel Review · Hackathon Readiness Audit

LaunchPad AI — Startup Intelligence Platform

A ten-seat review — CTO, Head of Innovation, VC Partner, Startup Banking Executive, Solution Architect, Principal AI Engineer, UX Designer, Product Manager, Security Architect, Hackathon Judge — against the standard laid out for a global discovery-and-ranking platform.

SUBJECT — cross-border payments MVP, live at Cloud Run DATA MODE — synthetic, single profile VERDICT — see §11
Read this before the scorecard

The review brief describes a platform that does not exist yet, and this panel will not pretend otherwise. It asks us to verify global sector search, live multi-source discovery, autonomous enrichment agents, and a ranked universe of startups. What is actually deployed — by explicit, agreed scope, given a same-day build window — is the single hero use case: one synthetic company (NovaTrade AI GmbH), scored deterministically for a cross-border-payments opportunity, narrated by one guarded LLM call, released only after a human approves it.

That is not a small gap. It is close to the entire discovery half of the brief. The panel's job below is to say, section by section, exactly what stands and exactly what is missing — not to soften either finding.

01

Executive Review

Seats: CTO · Head of Innovation · VC Partner · Startup Banking Executive

Everyone at this end of the table read the same live demo two ways at once. As a piece of engineering delivered in about a day, with a working deploy pipeline and a defensible governance pattern, it is genuinely impressive. As the thing its name promises — a platform that discovers and ranks startups — it has not started.

"I can approve a scoring methodology for a company I already know about. I cannot approve a sourcing strategy I've never seen run."CTO
"The part everyone underrates is the trust layer — mandatory human sign-off, a full audit trail, an explicit list of what the system will not claim. That's the actual blocker to bank adoption of AI, and it's handled well here. But 'AI-powered digital twin' in the deck implies enrichment happening today that isn't."Head of Innovation
"I look at hundreds of deals a year. A tool that scores one hand-entered company doesn't touch my sourcing problem. The seven-factor weighting itself reads like a reasonable v1 of an internal scorecard — just disconnected from any pipeline of deals to run it against."VC Partner
"This is the exact shape of brief I'd want on a client I'm already covering: why-now, evidence, suggested questions, product themes. Feed it my real book of business and it's useful tomorrow. It does nothing yet for a client I haven't met."Startup Banking Executive

Panel consensus: strong proof-of-concept for the governance and decision-support half of the vision; no proof-of-concept yet for the discovery half, which is the harder and more differentiating half.

02

Architecture Review

Seats: Solution Architect · Principal AI Engineer · Security Architect

Backend is FastAPI + Pydantic v2 over Firestore, with a repository interface swapped between an in-memory store (tests) and Firestore (deployed) — a clean, appropriately small pattern for this scope. Scoring is pure Python, isolated from the one point where an LLM is called. CI/CD is GitHub Actions building a Docker image and deploying to Cloud Run via Workload Identity Federation — no static service-account keys anywhere, which is the right default and not always the default hackathon teams choose.

Holds up Deterministic scoring is fully unit-tested (22 tests: determinism, threshold math, missing-data handling, RBAC, guardrails) and was verified against the real Firestore database directly, not just the in-memory test double.
Overclaimed if presented as-is "Agentic AI" appears once in the entire runtime: a single Gemini call that rephrases two prose fields, wrapped in a substring-matching banned-phrase filter. There is no agent framework, no tool use, no retrieval, no memory, no orchestration between autonomous agents — the seven "agent" stages in the design doc are, in the deployed code, ordinary function calls in a pipeline. See §5.

Build the ingestion layer first; the rest of the stack can stay exactly this small for a while longer.

03

Business Review

Seats: Startup Banking Executive · Product Manager

Every recommendation the system produces does answer "why engage" — the RM brief's product_themes field ties the score directly to concrete propositions (multi-currency accounts, virtual accounts, collections, FX exposure review, cash-visibility tooling). That traceability from evidence → score → product theme is real and is the single most bank-relevant design decision in the build.

Gap It only ever surfaces one opportunity type — cross-border payments and cash management — because that is the only scoring model implemented. Trade finance, credit, venture debt, accelerator partnership, and investment-banking referral opportunities exist in the source design document's own priority list but have no scoring logic behind them yet.
Gap No pipeline-value rollup is exposed to the executive. The revenue-potential driver scores exist under the hood but never surface as an estimated deal size or expected wallet share anywhere in the UI.

Product Manager's note: this is the right sequencing decision for a hackathon — one opportunity type done honestly beats nine done shallowly — but the pitch needs to say "opportunity type 1 of 9, fully working end to end" rather than implying breadth that isn't there.

04

UI Review

Seat: UX Designer, sitting as a bank executive with 30 seconds

The actual flow: sign in → a portfolio list (today, one card) → click through to Overview, a seven-way collapsible Scorecard, an RM Brief, and an Audit trail as separate tabs. Nothing about this is broken; it is analyst-detail information architecture wearing an executive-summary job.

30-second test: fails today Not because any single screen is bad — because there is no single glance that contains score, band, top opportunity, and top risk together. The Scorecard tab is the most detailed screen and the least scannable one; it is also the one an executive lands on first.
Worth keeping exactly as-is The "what this brief does not claim" section on the RM Brief tab. Most fintech dashboards never show their own boundaries; this one lists them in plain language next to the recommendation. Color-coded priority banding (high / monitor / validate / no-action) is also a clean, already-correct semantic pattern — don't touch it, extend it.

Absent entirely: a search bar, sector or geography filters, a map, a side-by-side comparison view, and any single confidence indicator that rolls the seven sub-scores into one number an executive reads without opening anything.

One-line fix that changes the grade: a summary strip above the tabs — final score, band, top opportunity, single top risk — turns this from "readable in five minutes" into "readable in thirty seconds," using data the backend already returns.

05

Agent Review

Seat: Principal AI Engineer

The design document names seven pipeline stages "agents." Reviewed against what actually runs in production, one of them touches an LLM at all.

1–2 · Signal Ingestion & Digital Twin

Reality
Not agents — a hardcoded seed loader and an in-memory context object. No live source, no scheduling, no retry, because there is nothing to retry against.
Verdict
not built as agents — this is the work described in §7/§9 as the actual next build.

3 · Decathlon / Opportunity Scoring

Purpose
Turn profile + payment + pain-point + signal data into 7 weighted sub-scores and a final band.
Input / Output
Structured records in → a fully-explained ScoreRecord out (every driver carries a rationale).
Dependencies
None external — pure Python, zero network calls.
Failure handling
Missing mandatory data is flagged, never silently scored as zero; can force a lower band even if the raw math clears the threshold.
Confidence / Retry / Fallback
Confidence band per sub-score; nothing to retry (deterministic); no fallback needed.
Verdict
real, and the strongest component in the system — but it is a scoring function, not an agent in any sense an AI engineer would use the word.

4 · Recommendation narrative

Purpose
Rephrase two already-computed prose fields (client summary, why-now) into fluent text.
Input / Output
Deterministic template text in → LLM prose out, or the same template if the model call fails.
Dependencies
Vertex AI Gemini — a soft dependency by design.
Failure handling
Confirmed working: on any Vertex error, silently reverts to the deterministic template. The system's correctness never depends on this call succeeding.
Confidence / Retry
No confidence score on the LLM output itself; single attempt, then fallback — no retry loop.
Human review
hard-gated — cannot reach client-facing status without an authenticated RM approval. This is the standout governance decision in the whole build.

6 · Trust & Control

Purpose
Block regulated-advice or guaranteed-outcome language before it can ship.
Reality
A fixed list of banned substrings ("guaranteed," "suitable for you," "credit approval," …), case-insensitive.
Failure mode
Trivially bypassable by paraphrase — "this should pay off nicely" would sail through what "guaranteed" would catch.
Verdict
real but shallow — good instinct, needs either a broader phrase list or a lightweight semantic classifier before any real deployment.

7 · Audit & Learning

Audit half
real — 100% of scoring, recommendation, and approval events are logged with actor and timestamp.
Learning half
not built — weights are proposed and activated by a human through an API; nothing yet calibrates them from RM feedback or outcomes.

What's actually missing, if the brief's discovery vision is the target: a Source Discovery agent (search by sector across live APIs), an Extraction/Normalization agent (unstructured text → structured signal), a Deduplication agent (same company found via two sources), and a Corroboration agent (don't trust a signal until ≥2 independent sources agree). None exist today. Do not build all four at once — Source Discovery plus Deduplication is the pair that unlocks the most credibility per unit of effort.

What should not be added: more pipeline stages for their own sake. Six of the seven "agents" here could correctly be described as one scoring function, one narrow LLM call, and a logger — that is the right amount of complexity for what this does, and a bank's model-risk function will prefer fewer, well-understood components to a sprawling agent graph.

06

Data Quality Review

Seat: Principal AI Engineer · Solution Architect

There is exactly one record of substance in the system: a hand-authored synthetic profile for "NovaTrade AI GmbH," with hand-authored confidence values (0.7–0.9) on its expansion signals rather than confidence computed from actual source reliability.

"Live data" claim False today, and worth stating plainly rather than hedging: nothing in the system refreshes on its own. A Firestore document changes only when a human calls the API again. Staleness is not yet the right risk to worry about, because there is no live feed old enough to go stale — the honest framing is "pre-live," not "live with gaps."
Duplicate risk No deduplication logic exists anywhere. Two different startup_id values for the same real company would silently coexist and could receive different scores with no cross-check.

None of this is a defect in what was built — it is the correct description of a system that has not yet reached the discovery stage. It should be described that way in the room, not glossed over.

08

Critical Bugs

Seat: Security Architect · Solution Architect

Found and already fixed, live, during this build — kept here for the audit trail, not as open items: Firestore rejected the weight-config data (nested arrays aren't supported by Firestore and had to be remodeled); an audit/score query required a composite index that hadn't been provisioned, worked around by sorting in Python instead; the /healthz path was silently intercepted ahead of the app on this project's Cloud Run domain and was moved to /api/health.

Still open:

  • CORS is wide open (allow_origins=["*"]) and the service is deployed publicly unauthenticated — acceptable for a synthetic demo, not for anything beyond it.
  • JWT secret regenerates on every deploy rather than living in Secret Manager — every redeploy silently signs everyone out. Fine for a hackathon, a real rough edge the moment this runs continuously.
  • Guardrail is substring-only — see §5, easily bypassed by paraphrase.
  • One driver table (control & implementation feasibility) is an invented, documented assumption — the source design document names this scoring dimension but never publishes its weights the way it does for the other six; a bank would need to formally bless these five weights before trusting the "high priority" threshold in a real decision.
09

Quick Wins

Ranked by impact per hour of remaining hackathon time. No new infrastructure — everything here uses what is already deployed.

  1. Seed 4–6 more diverse synthetic startups through the existing POST /profiles endpoint, spanning different bands (high / monitor / validate). This is the single highest-leverage change available: it turns the portfolio screen from a one-item list into an actual ranked comparison, using only what already works today.
  2. Add a one-line executive summary strip above the detail tabs — score, band, top opportunity, top risk — directly answering the 30-second critique in §4.
  3. Restrict CORS to the deployed frontend origin instead of *.
  4. Move the JWT secret into Secret Manager with a fixed value instead of regenerating it on every deploy, so sessions survive a redeploy during the demo day.
  5. Extend the guardrail phrase list with a handful of obvious paraphrases ("confident this will," "low-risk investment," …) — ten minutes of work, meaningfully lowers the worst-case live-demo slip.
  6. Fix the pitch language, not the code: describe this as a governed scoring-and-advisory workflow with one narrow, guarded LLM call — not an agentic discovery platform. A technically literate judge will respect a scoped MVP and penalize an overclaimed one.

The Verdict

"If this application were demonstrated today in front of senior banking executives, would they trust the recommendations enough to initiate engagement with the identified startups?"

No.

Not because the scoring is unreliable — because there is only one company in the system, and it's synthetic. There is nothing yet to "initiate engagement" with, in the sense the question asks. A narrower, honest yes is available: executives could reasonably trust the decision-support mechanics — transparent scoring, mandatory human sign-off, a complete audit trail — as a credible pattern worth funding to build out against real data and real discovery. That is a real answer, just a smaller one than the brief assumes.

What must change before any such demonstration lands honestly:

  1. Reframe the pitch as "a trust-first scoring and advisory workflow for a proven opportunity type," not "AI discovers and ranks the world's startups" — the build cannot support the second claim and an alert executive finds the gap within two minutes.
  2. Run Quick Win #1 before anyone opens the dashboard — seed several diverse profiles so there is an actual ranking on screen, not a single card.
  3. Say plainly, in the room, what "AI agent" means here: one narrow, guarded, fallback-safe LLM call for prose, nothing more — which is not a weakness to hide but the same governance principle the source design document itself insists on: decision support, never autonomous advice.
